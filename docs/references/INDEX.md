## blastfoam-guide
- File: BlastFoam_User_Guide.pdf (text: BlastFoam_User_Guide.txt; `=== page N ===` markers match the printed page numbers)
- Covers: blastFoam Theory and User Guide, Version 6.0 (Heylmun, Vonk, Shields, Brewer; Synthetik Applied Technologies, 30 Aug 2022), for OpenFOAM-9. Case structure, solvers, EOS, boundary conditions, post-processing. Chapters: governing equations (single- and multi-fluid, sec. 2); interfacial and granular models (3-4); thermodynamic models and equations of state, including idealGas, stiffenedGas, JWL, JWLC, BKW, tabulated (5.1-5.2); detonating thermodynamic model with activation models (none, linear, programmedIgnition, pressureBased, ArrheniusRate, 5.6.1) and afterburn models (constant, linear, Miller, 5.6.2); initialisation (5.9); turbulence (6); burst patches (7); region models and their boundary conditions (8); flux evaluation: HLL, HLLC, HLLCP, AUSM+, AUSM+up, Kurganov/Tadmor (10.1); time integration: Euler, RK2-RK4 SSP (10.3); AMR error estimators (cellSize, fieldValue, delta, densityGradient, Lohner) and load balancing (11); numerics and lookup tables (12); function objects: blastProbes, fieldMax, overpressure, impulse, timeOfArrival, Schlieren (15); utilities: setRefinedFields, blastToVTK, createTimeSeries, mergeProbes, calculateImpulse, initializeAtmosphere, rotateFields, reconstructParAll (16); fvModels/fvConstraints (17); solvers: blastFoam, blastEulerFoam, blastFSIFoam and the experimental ones (19); worked examples, including double Mach reflection, two-fluid shock tube, two-charge case, buried charge, bursting windows (20).
- Use for: any task that writes, edits or parses a blastFoam/OpenFOAM case (`blastlab/solvers/foam/writer.py`, `reader.py`, `runner.py`, `materials.py`); choosing or checking a solver, EOS, activation or afterburn model, flux scheme, time integrator, AMR error estimator or boundary condition; setRefinedFields input; probes, impulse, arrival time and other post-processing; auditing the code against the guide (`docs/verification/solver-options-review`, `blastfoam-claims`).
- Cite as: blastFoam User Guide v6.0, sec. 5.6.2.4 (p. 76)
- Keywords: blastFoam, JWL, equationOfState, thermodynamicProperties, activationModel, afterburn, Miller, fluxScheme, HLLC, AUSM+, RK2SSP, Lohner, densityGradient, errorEstimator, setRefinedFields, blastProbes, impulse, timeOfArrival, overpressure, reconstructParAll

## ufc-3-340-02
- File: ufc_3_340_02.pdf (text: ufc_3_340_02.txt). **Page markers are PDF page indices, not printed page numbers:** the PDF runs about 75-76 pages ahead, so printed p. 83 (Figure 2-7) is `=== page 159 ===`. The printed number sits alone on a line a few lines under each marker. Grep for the figure, table or paragraph number rather than a page. Most charts are images, so their text layer holds only axis labels. Read the PDF page itself for curve values.
- Covers: UFC 3-340-02, *Structures to Resist the Effects of Accidental Explosions*, 5 Dec 2008 (supersedes TM 5-1300 / NAVFAC P-397 / AFR 88-22, Nov 1990). Blast loads, Kingery-Bulmash curves, reflected pressure and impulse, SDOF response charts, dynamic increase factors, design of RC and steel elements. Ch. 1: explosion protection system, protection categories, human, equipment and explosive tolerances, pressure design ranges. Ch. 2: blast, fragment and shock loads. It includes TNT equivalency (2-9), free-air, air and surface bursts (2-13), with Figure 2-7 (spherical free-air, printed p. 83) and Figure 2-15 (hemispherical surface burst), plus reflected pressure and impulse against angle of incidence. Confined explosions follow: shock, gas and leakage pressures (2-14), then external blast loads on above-ground structures with and without openings, and pressure build-up inside (2-15). The chapter ends with primary and secondary fragments (2-16 to 2-19), ground and air shock, and shock response spectra (2-20 to 2-24). Ch. 3: principles of dynamic analysis. It covers resistance functions, equivalent SDOF load-mass factors and the maximum-response charts for elastic-plastic SDOF systems (Figure 3-49 onward, triangular load; Figure 3-64). Ch. 4: reinforced concrete design, with the DIF in 4-13.2 and Table 4-1. Ch. 5: structural steel design, with the yield and ultimate DIF in Figure 5-2 and Tables 5-2 and 5-3. Ch. 6: special considerations in explosive-facility design. Appendices per chapter give worked examples, symbols and bibliography.
- Use for: any task that computes or checks free-air, surface-burst or reflected blast parameters against Kingery-Bulmash (`tests/test_foam_kb_anchor.py`, the physics anchors in `docs/verification/`). Also for any task that turns a pressure-time history into a structural load or response: equivalent triangular load, SDOF, resistance, DIF. It covers confined or vented explosions, gas and leakage pressure, and loads on walls, roofs and openings, which is what the HD1 vent and wall work needs. Also for the terms and units of scaled distance, impulse and arrival time.
- Cite as: UFC 3-340-02 (2008), para. 2-15.3 / Figure 2-7 (printed p. 83)
- Keywords: Kingery-Bulmash, scaled distance, Z = R/W^(1/3), free air burst, surface burst, hemispherical, reflected pressure, reflected impulse, angle of incidence, TNT equivalency, confined explosion, gas pressure, leakage pressure, vented, SDOF, resistance function, load-mass factor, dynamic increase factor, DIF, positive phase duration

## llnl-explosives-handbook
- File: LLNL_Explosives_Handbook_UCRL-52997.pdf (text: LLNL_Explosives_Handbook_UCRL-52997.txt). Downloaded 2026-09-25 from `https://archive.org/download/LLNLExplosivesHandbook/LLNL%20Explosives%20Handbook.pdf`. **A scanned document: the text layer is OCR and garbles numbers** (e.g. "Comp C-A" for C-4, a PETN E0 read as "O o d"). Never take a value from the `.txt`. Read it off the page image. Table 8-7 is pre-rendered as `LLNL_Explosives_Handbook_UCRL-52997_table8-7_p8-22.png` and `..._p8-23.png`. `=== page N ===` markers are PDF indices, and Table 8-7 is PDF pages 229-230 (printed 8-22 / 8-23).
- Covers: B. M. Dobratz and P. C. Crawford, *LLNL Explosives Handbook: Properties of Chemical Explosives and Explosive Simulants*, UCRL-52997, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, 31 Jan 1985 (change 2). Its chapters give composition, physical and thermal properties, sensitivity, and detonation properties. Ch. 8 (detonation properties) has C-J parameters and the **JWL equation of state** (sec. 8.3.1, **Table 8-7**, pp. 8-22/8-23): ρ0, P_CJ, D, E0 (per unit volume, Mbar·cm³/cm³), Γ, A, B, C, R1, R2, ω for TNT, Comp C-4, HMX, PETN (at 0.88, 1.26, 1.50 and 1.77 g/cm³), Comp B, cyclotol, octol, the LX and PBX series, NM, tetryl, pentolite and others. It also covers detonation energy and cylinder-test data (8.3.2, Table 8-8) and the Gurney method (8.4). Footnotes: a = no cylinder data, b = estimated quantity, c = non-ideal composite. **It has no RDX and no ANFO row.**
- Use for: any task that reads, adds or checks a JWL or C-J value in `blastlab/core/materials.py` (the primary source for TNT, C4, HMX and PETN, see TODO `P1-14`), `blastfoam_toolkit/docs/jwl_parameters.md`, or a blastFoam `thermophysicalProperties` JWL block; `programmedIgnition` Pcj; converting E0 per volume to e0 per mass (e0 = E0 / ρ0).
- Cite as: Dobratz & Crawford, LLNL Explosives Handbook, UCRL-52997 (1985), Table 8-7, p. 8-23
- Keywords: JWL, Jones-Wilkins-Lee, equation of state, C-J, Chapman-Jouguet, Pcj, detonation velocity, E0, detonation energy, R1, R2, omega, Gamma, TNT, C-4, HMX, PETN, cylinder test, Gurney, UCRL-52997

## viper-theory
- File: ViperTheoryManual_Client.pdf (text: ViperTheoryManual_Client.txt; 9 pages, `=== page N ===` = printed page)
- Covers: J. Wurster & P. McDonald, *Viper Blast Theory Manual*, Viper Applied Science, 21 Aug 2025 (client edition). Euler equations (2); EOS, ideal gas with hard-coded gamma = 1.4 and JWL (2.1, eq. 2.10); AUSMDV scheme after Rose [2001] / Air3D (3.1); transmitting, reflecting and terminating boundaries (3.2); obstacles as whole cells (3.3); 1D spherical / 2D axisymmetric / 3D and remaps (3.4); no viscosity (3.5); afterburn as an S-curve energy addition (3.6); references and a list of papers that use Viper. **It contains no material data:** no JWL table and no ANFO or RDX values. It says Viper ships default values per explosive, and points to the User Manual for them and to Lee, Hornig & Kury (1968), UCRL-50422, OSTI 4783904, for the JWL form.
- Use for: comparing blastlab's methods with a second airblast CFD code (scheme, boundaries, remap chain, afterburn model); **not** a source for any material constant (P1-14).
- Cite as: Wurster & McDonald, Viper Blast Theory Manual (2025), sec. 2.1, p. 4
- Keywords: Viper, Viper Blast, AUSMDV, Air3D, Rose, JWL, ideal gas, gamma 1.4, remap, afterburn, S-curve, transmitting boundary, reflecting boundary, terminating boundary, obstacles
